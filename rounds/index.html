Put a few notes on the wheel. Each time it comes around, another voice joins — following you a quarter turn behind, a third above, never quite catching up. A four-note idea becomes a five-voice round, and you only ever wrote the one line.

One line, heard from five places.

Nothing here is generated on your behalf. Every voice is playing the exact phrase you drew — just arriving late, and sometimes a couple of scale degrees higher. That is the whole trick of a round, and it is why a round sounds composed when it is only delayed.

The pitches are a minor pentatonic, five notes to the octave, so no two voices can land on a semitone and no combination can go sour. The instrument is allowed to add harmony without permission; it should never be able to punish you for letting it.

The wheel turns on the audio clock rather than the animation frame, so the light and the sound cannot drift apart. Your phrase stays in this browser and is waiting when you come back.
